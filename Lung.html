🫁 Lung Ultrasound

Whangārei ED POCUS Training

1. Where to Scan

Perform a systematic bilateral lung examination. A simple six-point approach examines three regions on each side.

  • R1 / L1: anterior chest
  • R2 / L2: lateral chest
  • R3 / L3: posterolateral / PLAPS region

Use a curvilinear or phased-array probe for the general survey. A linear probe is useful when detailed assessment of the pleural line and lung sliding is required.

Lung surface anatomy. Source: POCUS 101.

Technique: optimise probe choice, depth and gain before deciding whether a finding is abnormal.

2. Normal Lung — Sliding and A-Lines

Identify:

  • Two rib shadows — the “bat sign”
  • The bright pleural line
  • Lung sliding with respiration
  • Horizontal repeating A-lines

Normal aerated lung with horizontal A-lines. Source: ACEP Sonoguide.

A-lines are horizontal reverberation artefacts associated with aerated lung.

3. B-Lines

B-lines are vertical artefacts arising from the pleural line and extending to the bottom of the screen.

  • Move with lung sliding
  • Erase or interrupt A-lines
  • Multiple bilateral B-lines may indicate interstitial syndrome
B-LINES ULTRASOUND IMAGE
Pitfall: B-lines are not synonymous with heart failure. Interpret their number, distribution and pleural appearance in the clinical context.

4. Pleural Effusion and the Spine Sign

Examine the lung bases through the liver or spleen and identify the diaphragm.

In normally aerated lung the vertebral bodies are not visible above the diaphragm. Fluid or consolidated lung allows ultrasound to transmit further into the thorax.

PLEURAL EFFUSION / SPINE SIGN IMAGE
Seeing the spine continue above the diaphragm is the spine sign.

5. Pneumonia and Consolidation

Consolidated lung becomes tissue-like rather than predominantly artefactual.

  • Tissue-like or “hepatized” lung
  • Irregular shred sign
  • Air bronchograms
  • Dynamic air bronchograms can support pneumonia
PNEUMONIA / CONSOLIDATION IMAGE

6. Pneumothorax

Look for:

  • Absent lung sliding
  • Absence of B-lines at the point being examined
  • Barcode / stratosphere sign on M-mode
  • A lung point, when present
PNEUMOTHORAX / LUNG POINT IMAGE
Important: absent lung sliding alone does not diagnose pneumothorax. Other conditions can abolish or markedly reduce sliding.

7. Artefacts and Pitfalls

  • A-lines: horizontal reverberation artefact
  • B-lines: vertical artefacts arising from the pleura
  • Mirror-image artefact: commonly seen across the diaphragm
  • Curtain sign: normally aerated lung moves over the upper abdominal organs with inspiration
MIRROR IMAGE / CURTAIN SIGN
Lung ultrasound predominantly detects pathology that reaches the pleural surface. A normal lung ultrasound does not exclude all pulmonary disease.
